# International Data Transfers

> **Draft for legal review — version {{VERSION}}.** Prepared as a working template. Before publishing, have a lawyer qualified in each country where you operate review and adapt it, and fill every [bracketed] item.

1. **Where data is stored.** Primary hosting: **[country/region]**. Backups: **[country/region]**.
2. **Nigeria (NDPA Part VIII, ss.41–43; GAID).** Transfers out of Nigeria rely on an adequacy decision by the NDPC where available, otherwise on contractual clauses or other appropriate safeguards recognised by the NDPC, documented in a transfer record. Institutions that require in-country hosting can request a Nigeria-hosted deployment.
3. **EU/UK (GDPR Chapter V).** Transfers rely on adequacy decisions or on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (UK: the International Data Transfer Addendum), with a transfer impact assessment. If we offer the service to people in the EU without an EU establishment, we appoint an EU representative (art.27): **[name]**.
4. **Kenya (DPA 2019, ss.48–50).** Transfers require appropriate safeguards and documented proof; sensitive data transfers require consent and safeguards. Some processing for strategic interests may need local storage — check before onboarding a Kenyan public institution.
5. **South Africa (POPIA s.72).** Transfers only to recipients bound by laws, binding corporate rules or agreements giving adequate protection, or with consent.
6. **Ghana (Act 843).** Processing must comply with the Act and the Data Protection Commission's requirements for data processed outside Ghana.
7. **Our commitments.** Same security everywhere; challenge unlawful access requests; tell the institution about any government request unless legally prohibited; keep a register of transfers.
