**SOS Connect is a campus communication and alerting tool. It is not a replacement for national emergency services.** If you can, always call the national emergency number (in Nigeria **112**; Lagos also **767**; Ghana **112**; Kenya **999/112**; South Africa **10111/112**) or your campus security line.

## How SOS works
- **With internet:** your alert goes straight to your institution's responders (campus security and others it appoints) and appears on their phones and the web console.
- **At the same time, with or without internet:** your phone sends the alert to nearby phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. They show it to members nearby and pass it on. The first phone in the chain that has internet delivers it to responders.
- **No data at all:** the app offers to send an ordinary SMS to campus security with your location link.

## What we cannot guarantee
Delivery and speed depend on things we do not control: whether other phones are nearby and have the app open (iPhones relay only while the app is open), Bluetooth/Wi-Fi/location being switched on, battery, buildings blocking signal, and whether responders are on duty. Alerts may be delayed or may not arrive. Location from GPS can be wrong by tens of metres, especially indoors.

## Responsibilities of the institution
Each institution decides who responds, staffs its responder roles, sets its campus security number in the app, and remains responsible for its own emergency procedures. Unanswered alerts are escalated to senior roles after 2 minutes.

## False alerts
Raising a false SOS on purpose is a serious breach of the Acceptable Use Policy and may also break your institution's rules and the law.

## Your data in an SOS
Your name, contact details, your note and (if you allowed it) your location are shared with responders and with members near you who receive the alert. See the Privacy Policy.
